By the WellWater HQ team

A well water pump replacement costs $975 to $2,575 on average in 2026, with most homeowners landing around $1,775, according to LawnStarter's 2026 cost data. A separate 2026 estimate puts the range at $1,500 to $2,827. Both surveys agree on the biggest cost driver: well depth, which adds roughly $500 to $1,000 for every additional 100 feet.

Pump type matters almost as much as depth. A jet pump for a shallow well costs a fraction of a submersible pump rated for a deep, high-output well, and the two aren't interchangeable once you're past about 100 feet of well depth.

## What's the average cost to replace a well pump?

Expect $975 to $2,827 for a typical residential replacement, spanning both major 2026 cost surveys. Where you land in that range depends mostly on pump type, horsepower, and well depth, not the brand name on the pump itself.

## Submersible vs jet pump: which do you need, and what's the price difference?

A jet pump sits above ground and pulls water up through suction, which limits it to wells shallower than about 100 feet; installed cost runs $400 to $1,400. A submersible pump sits down in the well itself and pushes water up, which handles deeper wells and higher flow rates; installed cost runs $1,000 to $2,500 for wells in the 150 to 300 foot range, climbing to $1,800 to $3,000 for a 200-foot well and $4,000 to $6,000 or more for a 400-foot well.

The depth of your well, not preference, generally decides which pump type you need. Past roughly 100 feet, a jet pump can no longer reliably lift water, and a submersible pump becomes the only practical option.

## How does well depth change the price?

| Well depth | Typical pump type | Installed cost range |
|---|---|---|
| Under 100 feet | Jet pump | $400 to $1,400 |
| 150 to 300 feet | Submersible | $2,500 to $4,500 |
| 300 feet or more | Heavy-duty submersible | $5,000 to $8,000+ |

Every additional 100 feet of depth adds roughly $500 to $1,000 in pipe, wire, and labor, since deeper wells need longer drop pipe, more electrical wire run down the casing, and more labor time to pull and reset the string of pipe.

## What determines pump horsepower, and how does HP affect cost?

Horsepower is set by how much water you need to move and how far it has to travel vertically, not household size alone. A basic 1/2 HP submersible unit costs $300 to $600, a 3/4 HP unit runs $500 to $900, a 1 HP unit runs $700 to $1,400, and larger 1.5 HP and 2 HP units used for deep or high-demand wells run $900 to $2,500.

Undersizing horsepower to save money upfront is a common mistake: a pump working past its rated capacity burns out faster, which turns a cheaper unit into a more expensive one over its shortened lifespan.

## What's included in a full installation, beyond the pump itself?

A complete submersible pump replacement includes the pump unit, drop pipe and fittings sized to well depth, electrical wire, and labor to pull the old pump and set the new one. Drop pipe and fittings alone typically add $300 to $800 to the project depending on depth, on top of the pump unit's own $400 to $1,500 cost range.

## When does a pump need replacing versus repairing?

Penn State Extension recommends periodic well system checks as part of routine well maintenance, since catching a failing pump early can mean a repair instead of a full replacement ([Penn State Extension, Protecting Your Water Well](https://extension.psu.edu/protecting-your-water-well)). Signs a pump is failing rather than just needing a minor fix include a sharp drop in water pressure, the pump cycling on and off rapidly, air sputtering from faucets, or a noticeable jump in your electric bill from a motor working harder than it should.

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to replace a well pump?**
$975 to $2,827 on average in 2026 across current cost surveys, with well depth and pump horsepower as the two biggest variables. Shallow jet-pump wells fall at the low end; deep submersible installations fall at the high end.

**Is a submersible pump more expensive than a jet pump?**
Yes, generally. Jet pumps install for $400 to $1,400, while submersible pumps run $1,000 to $2,500 or more depending on depth, since submersible installation involves more pipe, wire, and labor to reach the pump in the well.

**How much does depth add to well pump replacement cost?**
Roughly $500 to $1,000 per additional 100 feet of depth, driven by longer drop pipe, more wire, and additional labor time.

**How long does a well pump last?**
A well-maintained submersible pump commonly lasts 15 to 25 years, though heavy sediment, iron, or a pump working beyond its rated horsepower can shorten that considerably.

**What size pump horsepower do I need?**
It depends on your well's depth and your household's peak demand in GPM, not just square footage. A licensed well contractor sizes horsepower against both factors together; undersizing shortens the pump's life.

Bottom line: get a quote that separates pump unit cost, drop pipe and wire, and labor rather than one bundled number. That breakdown is what lets you tell whether $2,500 for a 250-foot submersible job is fair or inflated.
